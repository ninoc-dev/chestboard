ChestBoard

When your adventure needs a little push

Chestboard propelled system

ChestBoard™ Classic

ChestBoard™ Classic is the basic version of the propelled system, designed to stabilize and support paraplegic and quadriplegic divers.

Its main function is to allow for even distribution of body weight in water and ensure stability during movement. It is suitable for those who desire additional support during swimming and diving without advanced features for increasing speed.

ChestBoard™ Flow

ChestBoard™ Flow is designed for divers who require improved hydrodynamics and greater flexibility in movement. Its unique shape includes specially designed channels that reduce water resistance and allow for better maneuvering in various underwater conditions.

This model is an excellent choice for divers who want faster and more efficient movement through the water. ChestBoard™ Flow is ideal for those who often encounter strong currents.

ChestBoard™ Power

ChestBoard™ Power is specifically designed for divers who need the maximum possible power when moving through the water. Its unique structure allows for better propulsion with minimal effort.

ChestBoard™ Power is the perfect choice for anyone who wants maximum performance and speed while swimming. The system allows for maximum utilization of body strength and hydrodynamics and is an excellent choice for researchers, athletes, and professional divers.

Research and Planning

The journey began with a deep dive into the world of adaptive diving, focusing especially on the needs of individuals with spinal cord injuries. We quickly realized that to create a truly useful product, we needed to understand not just the technical requirements, but also the lived experiences of our future users. Our team spent weeks combing through scientific literature, case studies, and technical manuals, learning about the biomechanics of movement in water, the challenges of buoyancy control, and the psychological barriers faced by people with limited mobility. We also looked at existing solutions on the market, analyzing their strengths and weaknesses, and identifying gaps that our product could fill.

Beyond academic research, we reached out to a wide network of professionals. We scheduled interviews and informal discussions with rehabilitation doctors, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, and diving instructors who had experience working with people with disabilities. These conversations were invaluable, as they highlighted practical issues that are often overlooked in theory—such as the importance of easy donning and doffing, the need for quick-release mechanisms in emergencies, and the psychological comfort that comes from feeling secure and in control underwater. We also learned about the regulatory landscape, including safety standards and certification requirements for adaptive diving equipment.

Parallel to this, we began building relationships with companies specializing in diving gear and assistive devices. We visited manufacturing facilities, attended trade shows, and even participated in workshops where we could see firsthand how materials like high-density foams, lightweight alloys, and corrosion-resistant plastics were being used in the latest products. These experiences gave us a clearer sense of what was technically feasible and what innovations might be possible. By the end of this phase, we had a comprehensive understanding of the challenges ahead and a clear set of objectives: to design a product that was not only safe and functional, but also adaptable, user-friendly, and accessible to a broad range of people.

This initial phase laid the foundation for everything that followed. The insights we gained from research, professional consultations, and industry observations shaped our vision and strategy. We emerged from this stage with a strong sense of purpose, a network of expert contacts, and a detailed plan for moving forward into the prototyping and development stages. The groundwork was set for a project that would not only address a technical challenge but also make a meaningful difference in the lives of its users.

Prototype Development and Collaboration with Experts

Armed with a wealth of information, we moved into the prototyping phase. Our first prototypes were intentionally simple, focusing on the core concept of the ChestBoard—a device that could provide stability and support for divers with limited trunk control. We experimented with different shapes, sizes, and attachment systems, using everything from foam boards and Velcro straps to 3D-printed components and custom-molded plastics. Each iteration was subjected to rigorous bench testing, where we simulated the stresses and movements that the device would experience in real-world use.

Collaboration was at the heart of this phase. We organized a series of workshops and focus groups, inviting not only medical professionals and engineers, but also potential users and their families. These sessions were eye-opening, as participants shared candid feedback about comfort, usability, and aesthetics. We learned, for example, that even small details—like the placement of padding or the type of buckle used—could make a significant difference in user satisfaction. Based on this feedback, we made continuous improvements, refining the design to better meet the needs of our target audience.

At the same time, we began the process of documenting our work for regulatory approval. This involved creating detailed technical drawings, compiling safety data, and preparing user manuals that explained how to use the ChestBoard safely and effectively. We also started reaching out to potential partners for future clinical trials and field tests, laying the groundwork for the next stage of development. By the end of this phase, we had several working prototypes that were ready for real-world testing, as well as a growing network of supporters and collaborators who were invested in our success.

This stage was marked by a spirit of innovation and adaptability. Each challenge we encountered led to new ideas and solutions, and the feedback loop between our team and the broader community of experts and users became a driving force in our development process. The prototypes evolved rapidly, and with each iteration, we moved closer to a product that could truly make a difference in adaptive diving.

First Testing in Kranj

After months of theoretical work and hands-on prototyping, the moment of truth arrived: our first real-world test. On Thursday, February 12th, I set out with three team members for the Olympic swimming pool in Kranj, where we had arranged to meet Luka, Alenka, Yoda, and Branko. The drive there was filled with a mix of excitement and anxiety—would our hard work pay off, or would we discover unforeseen problems? Upon arrival, we meticulously checked all our equipment, making sure every component was in place and functioning as intended.

As we began assembling the setup, the tension in the air was palpable. Our ground crew—Luka, Jasha, and Lenart—worked together seamlessly, attaching the ChestBoard to Yoda’s chest while he sat in his wheelchair. Once everything was secured, we moved to the poolside, where Luka and Alenka prepared to film and assist underwater, while Branko took charge of coordinating the activities from above, his voice echoing across the pool as he issued instructions and encouragement. The first moments in the water were nerve-wracking, but as Yoda adjusted to the device, our confidence grew. The ChestBoard performed as expected, providing the necessary support and stability, and allowing Yoda to experience a sense of freedom and mobility that had previously been out of reach.

Over the course of four intense hours, we gathered a wealth of data—videos, photos, and firsthand observations. We tested the universal ChestBoard for both SCUBA and freediving, and even had time for Luka and Alenka to try the device in “apnea mode.” A serendipitous encounter with a random visitor, who turned out to be the renowned freediving champion and instructor Mr. Tadej Črnoš, added an unexpected highlight to the day. His positive feedback and professional insights not only validated our approach but also sparked new ideas for expanding the product’s applications beyond its original target group. By the end of the session, we were exhausted but exhilarated, knowing that we had taken a major step forward.

This first field test was a turning point for the project. It provided us with invaluable real-world feedback and revealed both strengths and areas for improvement. The experience also strengthened our team’s resolve and camaraderie, as we saw firsthand the impact our work could have. We left the pool with a renewed sense of purpose and a clear list of next steps for further development.

Second Testing and Further Development

Buoyed by the success of our first test, we left Kranj with a trove of materials and a renewed sense of purpose. The following two weeks were a whirlwind of activity as we reviewed the footage, analyzed the feedback, and brainstormed ways to improve the ChestBoard. We identified several areas for enhancement, including adjustments to the attachment system, refinements to the board’s shape, and improvements in comfort and ease of use. Drawing on these insights, we developed three new prototypes—two tailored for apnea and one for SCUBA diving—each incorporating the lessons learned from our initial trial.

Preparation for the second round of testing was even more thorough. We brought two different sizes of the ChestBoard for SCUBA, each attached to AP Diving Commando vests in sizes S and M, and ensured that the new attachment systems were robust and easy to operate. Our young assistants, Lenart and Anže, played a crucial role in setting up and supporting the underwater team, while Alenka and Luka meticulously documented every aspect of the process with photos and videos. Yoda, now more familiar with the equipment, was able to fully enjoy the experience, testing the device in both SCUBA and apnea modes and providing valuable feedback on its performance.

This second testing session was marked by greater efficiency and a deeper sense of teamwork. The improvements we had made were immediately apparent, with users reporting increased comfort and confidence in the water. We also began to explore new possibilities for the ChestBoard, such as adapting it for use by children or individuals with different types of mobility challenges. As the day drew to a close, we left Kranj with a clear roadmap for further refinements and a growing sense of excitement about the future. Our next destination: the legendary Y-40 pool, where we would put the ChestBoard to the ultimate test in a world-class diving environment.

With each round of testing and development, our vision for the ChestBoard became clearer and more ambitious. The feedback from users and professionals alike fueled our creativity and determination. We were no longer just building a product—we were building a community and a movement toward greater accessibility and inclusion in the world of diving.
